Abstract

Computer-generated holography (CGH) seeks to synthesize a complex wavefront on a spatial light modulator (SLM) whose free-space propagation reconstructs a target 3D scene. Existing CGH pipelines are tightly coupled to specific scene representations and provide limited support for geometry-aware light transport effects across complex 3D scenes. We introduce WaveCast, a unified ray–wave framework for hologram generation across heterogeneous volumetric and surface-based primitives, including 3D Gaussians and triangle meshes. At the core of WaveCast is wave casting, a ray-based process that traverses mixed geometry while accumulating transport-aware wave contributions associated with visibility, transmittance, reflection and refraction. These light–scene interactions are then converted into localized subhologram contributions that are coherently accumulated on the SLM. To enable scalable hologram synthesis, we develop a custom CUDA kernel for tiled gather-style subhologram accumulation, achieving interactive performance. Experiments on real and hybrid scenes demonstrate that, to the best of our knowledge, WaveCast is the first holographic rendering pipeline supporting hybrid real–virtual scenes and path-dependent optical effects. Our work establishes a foundation for scalable, geometry-aware holographic rendering where volumetric and surface-based scene representations can be jointly traversed and synthesized into coherent holograms.

How WaveCast works

Prior CGH pipelines reduce a scene to primitive positions before wave synthesis, which discards the information needed to express what happens along a light path. WaveCast instead resolves geometric transport first and carries path-level state into the wave stage: rays traced through the scene produce an ordered sequence of ray–primitive interactions, recording accumulated transmittance, interaction order, and reflective or refractive redirection. Each interaction becomes a localized wave emitter that is accumulated over a diffraction-limited subhologram footprint on the SLM plane.

Because the ray path survives into wavefront synthesis, the same pipeline represents effects that position-only formulations cannot model directly — reflection, refraction, and multi-bounce focus behavior, including the depth shifts they induce.

Two accelerations make it interactive

A naive implementation emits one wave contributor per ray hit, which is far too slow at HD resolution. WaveCast introduces a segment-level virtual source that approximates many volumetric interactions within a ray segment using a single equivalent emitter while preserving the dominant transmittance and depth structure, and a tile-based gather-style CUDA kernel that reorganizes accumulation around SLM tiles, avoiding the atomic-write contention produced by many overlapping subhologram footprints.

Three execution modes

Mode 1Per-hit emitters with scatter accumulation
Mode 2Per-segment virtual sources with scatter accumulation
Mode 3Per-segment virtual sources with tile-based gather

All three modes produce closely matching reconstructions, but their runtimes differ by orders of magnitude: 9.32 s (Mode 1) → 310 ms (Mode 2) → 92 ms (Mode 3). Mode 3 preserves the same coherent superposition as Mode 2 and produces identical reconstructions.

Reflection and refraction change not only local appearance but also the effective image depth of the observed content. The reflected object behind the mirror and the refracted content behind the glass sphere are sampled by ray casting at their true geometric locations, yet come into focus at shifted focal planes — exactly the behaviour a position-only hologram formulation cannot reproduce.

Interactive scene editing

Because WaveCast requires no per-scene optimization, virtual assets can be inserted, moved and re-parameterized while holograms are regenerated on the fly. Below, mesh objects are transformed and their material properties — including refractive index — are edited live, with the hologram and its reconstruction preview updating interactively.

Comparison with prior CGH pipelines

We compare against GWS (holograms from 2D Gaussian primitives), Mesh AWB (analytical wave-based synthesis on triangle meshes) and Mesh Silhouette (mesh synthesis with silhouette-based occlusion handling), under a matched per-scene primitive budget. For each example we show a focal reconstruction and zoom-in regions at a far and a near focus setting.
