Abstract

Computer-generated holography (CGH) seeks to synthesize a complex wavefront on a spatial light modulator (SLM) whose free-space propagation reconstructs a target 3D scene. Existing CGH pipelines are tightly coupled to specific scene representations and provide limited support for geometry-aware light transport effects across complex 3D scenes. We introduce WaveCast, a unified ray–wave framework for hologram generation across heterogeneous volumetric and surface-based primitives, including 3D Gaussians and triangle meshes. At the core of WaveCast is wave casting, a ray-based process that traverses mixed geometry while accumulating transport-aware wave contributions associated with visibility, transmittance, reflection and refraction. These light–scene interactions are then converted into localized subhologram contributions that are coherently accumulated on the SLM. To enable scalable hologram synthesis, we develop a custom CUDA kernel for tiled gather-style subhologram accumulation, achieving interactive performance. Experiments on real and hybrid scenes demonstrate that, to the best of our knowledge, WaveCast is the first holographic rendering pipeline supporting hybrid real–virtual scenes and path-dependent optical effects. Our work establishes a foundation for scalable, geometry-aware holographic rendering where volumetric and surface-based scene representations can be jointly traversed and synthesized into coherent holograms.

Methodology

WaveCast resolves geometric transport before wave synthesis and carries path-level state into it, so reflection, refraction and multi-bounce focus behaviour — including the depth shifts they induce — survive into the hologram.

For each ray leaving an SLM pixel we query the nearest mesh hit, which bounds the current segment. Gaussians inside that segment are processed in depth order together with the hit, weighted by front-to-back transmittance and accumulated online. A diffuse hit terminates the ray; a reflective or refractive one starts a new segment.

Subhologram footprints overlap heavily on the SLM, so a scatter implementation drowns in concurrent atomic writes — contention, not arithmetic, is the bottleneck. WaveCast reverses the accumulation: each SLM tile gathers only the emitters that overlap it, binned and sorted by tile so one CUDA block handles each tile.

All three modes produce closely matching reconstructions, and Mode 3 preserves the same coherent superposition as Mode 2 exactly — but their runtimes differ by orders of magnitude.

Interactive scene editing

Because WaveCast requires no per-scene optimization, virtual assets can be inserted, moved and re-parameterized while holograms are regenerated on the fly. Mesh objects are transformed and their material properties — including refractive index — are edited live, with the hologram and its reconstruction preview updating interactively.

The control panel toggles hologram rendering, switches the display between subhologram / amplitude / phase / reconstruction, and sets the hologram depth range and the reconstruction preview depth.

Comparison with prior CGH pipelines

We compare against GWS (holograms from 2D Gaussian primitives), Mesh AWB (analytical wave-based synthesis on triangle meshes) and Mesh Silhouette (mesh synthesis with silhouette-based occlusion handling), under a matched per-scene primitive budget. For each example we show a focal reconstruction and zoom-in regions at a far and a near focus setting.
